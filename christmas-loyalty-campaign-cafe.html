How to Run a Christmas Loyalty Campaign in Your Café (Step-by-Step)

Not fifty festive ideas. One campaign, built on the only fact about December that matters commercially: you do not need help filling it. January is the month that needs the help — so December is where you collect, and January is where you pay out.

Last updated 2 October 2026 · Set up in an afternoon · Every step checked against the live product

?

How do I run a Christmas loyalty promotion for my coffee shop? Run one campaign in two halves. Through December, customers earn — a bonus stamp, a festive scratch card, a card that fills faster than usual. Through January, they redeem — one clear reward, with a date it stops. One goal, one reward, one end date, and four messages across the two months. It costs you nothing until it is claimed, and it is claimed in the month you want the footfall.

The number this is built on

December is not your problem. January is.

Across Britain’s managed restaurant, pub and bar groups, December 2025 sales rose 2.9% year on year. The following January, like-for-like sales fell 0.1%. That is the sector as a whole — and it is not the interesting part.

The interesting part is the segment cafés sit in. In the same January, the on-the-go segment dropped 3.2%, against a sector that was broadly flat. The coffee-and-a-pastry trade did measurably worse in January than pubs and restaurants did. Dry January, wet weather and a tight month after a loose one all land hardest on the discretionary £3.40 you buy on the way to work.

So the swing from your best month to your worst is steeper than the headlines suggest, and it is steeper for you than for the restaurant down the road. That is the thing a Christmas campaign should be aimed at. Most of them are aimed at December, which is a bit like watering the one plant that is already thriving.

Why “earn in December, redeem in January” beats a festive discount

A December discount discounts demand you already have. The person buying eight coffees for the office on 19 December was going to buy them anyway; taking 20% off that transaction is a straight transfer from your margin to theirs, in your highest-volume week. And the customer it genuinely attracts — the one who came because it was cheap — is the one least likely to come back in February.

A reward has a different shape. It costs you nothing at the moment you promise it, and you get to choose when it can be claimed. Promise it in December, make it claimable in January, and you have moved the cost out of your peak and the footfall into your trough, using the same budget.

There is also a well-documented reason the December half works at all. In a study of a real café reward programme, Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng found that customers buy coffee more frequently the closer they are to earning a free one — and that a card which starts with bonus stamps already on it gets completed faster than an identical-effort card that starts empty. December gives you six weeks of unusually heavy footfall to push as many people as possible towards the end of a card. January is where you collect on it.

The practical consequence: your December target is not free drinks given away. It is the number of customers sitting one or two stamps from the end on 1 January.

The shape of it

One campaign, two halves

Write these two boxes down before you touch any settings. Everything below is just making them happen.

1–24 December

Earn

The job is momentum. Make the card fill faster than it normally does, and make joining frictionless while your door is busiest.

  • A festive scratch card on every completed card
  • A head start for anyone who joins in December
  • Two messages: launch, and a halfway nudge
2–31 January

Redeem

The job is footfall in specific weeks. One reward, genuinely dated, aimed at the people who got close in December.

  • A dated promotion with a redemption limit
  • Aimed at customers who completed a card in December
  • Two messages: “it is waiting”, and a last-week reminder

Why it stops on 24 December: the days between Christmas and New Year are not normal trading, and a campaign that is still running while you are closed or on holiday hours is a campaign nobody is explaining at the counter.

Before you set anything up

Pick one goal. Pick one metric. Write both on the same piece of paper.

This is the step people skip, and it is the reason last year’s promo cannot be judged. There are three honest goals for a festive campaign and they pull in different directions, so choose one.

Goal A — more members

You want December’s unusual footfall to leave something behind. The metric is new stamp cards started in December. Build the campaign around joining: the head start in step four matters most, the reward itself matters least.

Goal B — more visits per customer

You have plenty of members who collect two stamps and drift. The metric is cards completed in December. Build it around the scratch card and the halfway nudge.

Goal C — January footfall

Your December is fine and your January is frightening. The metric is promotion redemptions between 2 and 31 January. Build it around the January reward being worth walking in for, and spend your message budget in January rather than December.

If you cannot choose, choose C. It is the one that addresses the 3.2% above, it is the easiest to measure, and it is the only one of the three where you will know in February whether it worked.

Step by step

Setting it up in BaristaCard

Six steps, about an afternoon. Everything described here is live in the product today — where the obvious thing does not exist, it says so and gives you the route that does work.

Create the December deal
15 minutes
Venue settings › New Deal

Set the number of stamps, choose the reward, and customise the look of the card. Give it a festive name — it is the first thing a customer reads when they scan your QR, and “December Card” does more work than you would expect.

Keep the stamp count the same as your normal card, or one higher if you are using the head start in step four. Do not shorten it for Christmas: a shorter card hands out more free coffee in the month you least need to buy footfall.

Worth knowing: deals do not carry start and end dates. A deal runs until you change it. So the festive deal is one you create when December starts and retire afterwards — it is the January promotion in step three that does the dated half of the campaign. Put “retire the December card” in your own diary for the 27th.
Swap the scratch-card prizes for December
20 minutes · the festive bit
Deal settings › Scratch Cards

Turn scratch cards on and set how often they appear — one for every completed card, or less often. Then add your prizes: each one gets a name, a description and odds, and the odds must add up to 100%. Mix one real win with several small ones.

Extra Stamp is one of the prize types, and for this campaign it is the important one. A scratch card weighted towards extra stamps pushes customers towards finishing a card rather than just giving stock away — which is exactly the December target from the section above. A festive prize table might be one free drink at low odds, extra stamps at high odds, and a mince pie in the middle.

This is the one genuinely automatic piece of the campaign. Once configured, it runs without you, and the deal analytics page shows how many were played, how many won, and your prize redemption rate.

Keep it a surprise: a scratch card works because the moment is guaranteed and the prize is not. A “double stamp every Tuesday” is the opposite, and customers simply move a visit they were making anyway. There is more on why that distinction matters in which loyalty rewards actually bring customers back.
Create the January promotion, with dates
25 minutes · the half that pays
Owner portal › your café’s page › Promotions › + New Promotion

This is where the dates live. A promotion takes an optional date range, so set it to your January window, and it stops appearing once the end date passes.

Promotions target segments rather than everybody, and you can combine any of these conditions: a minimum or maximum Coffee Beans balance, a minimum loyalty level, a minimum number of completed stamp cycles, and activity in the last so-many days. For this campaign, completed stamp cycles is the one you want — it is how you aim the January reward at the people who actually did the collecting in December. Leave the other fields empty.

Then set the limits. There are two: a total cap (“first 50 customers”) and a per-customer limit (“once per customer”). You can also require a redemption code the customer shows the barista. A total cap is how you make the campaign’s worst case a number you can afford, and it is worth setting even if you expect never to reach it.

Eligible customers see it in a “Special Offers for You” section on your venue’s deal page, and the system sends one push notification the first time someone qualifies — once per promotion, so nobody gets spammed.

The timing trap: a 2–31 January window sounds generous, but if everyone redeems in the first week you have refilled the week that was already recovering and left the last three empty. If your January has a particular dead patch — usually the middle fortnight — set the window to that fortnight instead. A shorter, better-aimed window beats a long vague one.
Give December’s new members a head start
10 minutes
Promotions › New Promotion › New Member template

December brings people through your door who have never been in before. The research says a card that already has progress on it gets finished more often than an empty one, so the aim is for nobody to leave December holding a blank card.

There are two routes and they stack. The first is the New Member template, which pre-fills a promotion aimed at customers with 0–30 beans and allows up to three redemptions — a small welcome offer for newcomers specifically. Apply it, then adjust it; templates are only a starting point.

The second is a staff habit rather than a setting. There is no “pre-stamp new cards” switch. What works instead is to set the card one stamp longer than you want it and have the barista add stamp one while they are already scanning a new customer in. A nine-stamp card and a ten-stamp card with one already given ask for exactly the same nine purchases — but the second is a job begun rather than a job not started, and that framing is the thing the research measures.

Also worth knowing: the free Starter plan allows three gift rewards a month, which is the route for giving one named customer something by hand. It is not a volume tool — three is three. Save them for a complaint or a regular you want to keep.
Put the four broadcast dates in your diary
30 minutes to write, 2 minutes each to send
Broadcasts › choose audience › write › send

Choose a single venue or all of your locations, write a title and a message in the rich text editor, and send. Customers get it by email and in-app notification, and they can reply — replies come back grouped by customer in your broadcast history, so this is a conversation rather than a mailout.

There is no scheduler. A broadcast is delivered the moment you hit send. That is the single most important practical fact about this campaign: write all four messages in October, save them in a note, and put the four send dates in your own calendar with a reminder. Nothing will send them for you.

The free Starter plan allows one broadcast a week, which is exactly four across December — the campaign below is built to fit inside it. Basic, at £9 a month, raises that to three a week per venue if you want more room.

One thing it cannot do: broadcasts go to a venue, not to a segment. You cannot send “your January reward is waiting” only to the people holding an unredeemed reward — it goes to everyone. So write that message so it reads sensibly to someone who has nothing waiting: “if you finished a card in December, there’s something on your card” rather than “your reward expires Friday”. The targeted half of the campaign is the promotion in step three; broadcasts are the loudhailer. There is more on choosing between channels in wallet pass, text or email.
Brief the staff in one line
5 minutes, and then every handover

Write the campaign on a card by the till in one sentence a Saturday part-timer could read aloud mid-rush. Not a poster. One sentence.

Something like: “Collect through December, and if you fill a card you get a free drink to claim in January — want one?”

Then say it out loud at every handover for the first week. This is the step with the highest ratio of impact to effort in the whole list, and it is the one that gets skipped because it does not involve logging in to anything. A campaign nobody mentions at the counter is a campaign that did not happen.

Copy you can use

The four messages

Four broadcasts, one a week, which is what the free plan allows. Write them now, send them on the dates. Change the words to sound like you — the timing and the job each one does are the parts that matter.

Send Monday 1 December · Launch
Something for January, starting today

“December’s the busiest month of our year, and January is the quietest — so we’ve put the two together. Every card you fill between now and Christmas Eve earns a free [drink] you can claim any time in January. Same card, same stamps, just a better month to spend them in. Scratch cards are on too, so there’s a surprise behind every completed card.”

Its job: explain the whole campaign in one go, including why. Customers are more willing to take part in something whose logic they can see than in something that is simply free.

Send Monday 15 December · Halfway nudge
Nine days left to fill a card

“Nine days until Christmas Eve, which is when December stamps stop counting towards a January drink. If your card’s nearly there, now is the moment. If it is not, you have got nine days and we are open every one of them.”

Its job: this is the one that earns the most. Customers accelerate as they approach a reward, and a deadline makes the distance feel shorter. Keep it short and keep the date in it.

Send Friday 2 January · It is waiting
If you filled a card in December

“Happy New Year. If you filled a card with us in December, there is a free [drink] on your card waiting — open the app and you will see it under Special Offers. It is there until the 31st. And if you did not get there, the normal card is back and we are very much open.”

Its job: this goes to everyone at the venue, not only to people holding a reward, so it is written to read sensibly either way. That is deliberate — see the note in step five.

Send Monday 26 January · Last week
Five days left on your January drink

“Last week to use a December reward — it goes on the 31st. If you have one sitting there, this is your reminder. Cold enough out there to justify the large one.”

Its job: unclaimed rewards are worth nothing to either of you. This message exists purely to convert them, and it is the one most people forget to send.

Replace [drink] with the actual reward. Specific beats generic: “a free flat white” outperforms “a free hot drink”, which outperforms “a reward”.

February’s job

Avoid these

Five ways this goes wrong

The reward expires before the slump

The commonest one, and the most expensive. A reward earned in December that must be used by 31 December pays out entirely inside the month you were already busy, and leaves you nothing for January. The expiry date is the campaign. If you change one thing about last year’s promo, change this.

Too many rewards

A festive scratch card, and double stamps, and a free mince pie over £10, and a January voucher. Each one dilutes the others, none of them is memorable, and no member of staff can explain the lot. One earn mechanic and one reward. If you want a second idea, keep it for next year.

No end date, or a fake one

A campaign with no end date never becomes urgent, so it never changes behaviour. But an end date you then quietly extend is worse: you have taught your regulars that your deadlines are decorative, and the next one will not work either. Set a real date, put it in writing, and hold it.

A discount wearing a loyalty costume

“20% off all December” is not a loyalty campaign, and neither is a double stamp every single day. Both hand value to customers who were coming anyway, in the month you need it least. A reward is something earned across visits and claimed later; if yours can be had on the first visit with no return required, it is a discount. The reward-margin maths is the honest way to compare the two.

Nobody sends the last message

Three of the four broadcasts get sent, because the first three are exciting. The 26 January reminder gets forgotten, because by then you are in the middle of your worst month and the campaign feels like last year’s idea. That message is the one that converts the rewards still sitting unclaimed. Put a calendar alert on it now, in October, while you are still enthusiastic.

Questions

Frequently asked

What is a good Christmas promotion for a café?

One that collects something in December and pays it out in January. December is the month you are already busy — a discount then simply costs you margin on trade you would have taken anyway. A reward that customers earn across December and redeem in January moves the cost into your quietest weeks and brings the footfall with it. Keep it to one reward, one clear end date, and a mechanic a part-time barista can explain in a sentence.

Should cafés offer Christmas discounts or loyalty rewards?

Rewards, in December specifically. A discount in your busiest month discounts demand you already have, and it attracts the customer who came for the price rather than for you. A loyalty reward costs you nothing until it is claimed, and you can choose when it is claimable. The one case for a December discount is a quiet weekday afternoon you genuinely cannot fill, and even then a double stamp usually costs the same and leaves the customer holding something.

How do I keep Christmas customers coming back in January?

Give them something unfinished and something unclaimed. A half-filled stamp card is a job already begun, and an unredeemed reward with a January expiry date is a reason to walk in during the month nobody wants to go anywhere. The research on café reward cards is clear that customers buy faster the closer they are to the reward, so the aim in December is to get as many people as possible close to the end of a card rather than to hand out free drinks.

When should I start a Christmas loyalty campaign?

Set it up in the second half of October and launch it on 1 December. The setup itself takes an afternoon, but the parts that go wrong when rushed are the staff briefing and the printed sign by the till, and those want a couple of quiet weeks. Leaving it until the end of November means launching in the same week as your first properly busy Saturday.

Can I run a seasonal campaign on a digital stamp card?

Yes, with one thing worth knowing: in BaristaCard the dates live on promotions, not on the stamp card itself. A deal has a stamp count, a reward and a card design, and it runs until you change it. A promotion has an optional date range, eligibility rules and redemption limits. So the seasonal campaign is a December deal you create and retire by hand, plus a dated January promotion that does the time-limited half of the work.
