The hidden cost of a café loyalty app

The monthly fee on the pricing page is the smallest number in this decision. Here is the rest of it — set-up, VAT, till time, tie-ins and the cost of leaving — with real 2026 UK figures.

Last updated 9 September 2026 · All prices checked against the providers' own published pages

£

What does a café loyalty app really cost? Published UK software prices in 2026 run from £0 to about £152 a month. But the largest line in the true cost is usually not software at all — it is the extra seconds each loyalty transaction adds at the till. For a café doing 1,200 transactions a week at the April 2026 National Living Wage of £12.71 an hour, ten extra seconds per transaction works out at roughly £184 a month in staff time — more than any of the software fees. Add VAT, which several providers quote their headline price without, and the tie-in to a particular card-payment provider, and the number on the pricing page can account for well under half of what you actually spend.

The real bill

Five costs that are not on the pricing page

None of this is anyone hiding anything. These are simply costs that sit outside the box the price goes in — which is exactly why they get missed.

1. VAT on the headline price
Adds 20% · often not shown

Some loyalty providers quote to businesses and therefore quote excluding VAT. Loyalzoo, for instance, publishes its GROW plan at £57 + VAT a month and SMART at £127 + VAT. Those are really £68.40 and £152.40 leaving your bank account.

Whether that 20% is a real cost or a paperwork step depends on one thing: whether you are VAT registered. Above the £90,000 turnover threshold you reclaim it and the ex-VAT figure is the honest one. Below it — and plenty of single-site independents are below it — you cannot reclaim anything, and the ex-VAT price on the website is not a price you will ever pay. Check which number you are being shown before you compare two providers.

2. The plan the feature actually lives in
£25–£54/mo on Square

Where loyalty is a module inside a bigger platform, the advertised plan price and the price with loyalty switched on are different numbers. Square's UK pricing lists a Free plan at £0 a month and Plus from £29 — and then lists Loyalty separately, starting from £25 a month, because it is not included in either.

So the café owner who read "£0" is looking at £25 a month once loyalty is on, and at £54 if the tier they need for the rest of the shop is Plus. Ask which plan the loyalty feature is included in, not what the cheapest plan costs.

3. Seconds at the till
≈£92–£275/mo

This is the big one, and almost nobody counts it. Every loyalty transaction adds time at the counter — finding the customer, scanning a code, waiting for a phone to come out of a pocket, explaining the scheme to someone who has not seen it before.

Take a café serving 200 transactions a day, six days a week: 1,200 a week. At the National Living Wage of £12.71 an hour from April 2026, the arithmetic is unforgiving. Five extra seconds per transaction costs about £92 a month in staff time. Ten seconds, about £184. Fifteen seconds, about £275.

Even the best case is several times the software fee. This is why "how many taps does this take at the counter" is a more important question than "how much is it a month", and why a scheme staff can run without thinking is worth paying more for. It is also the cost that falls hardest in the morning rush, when the queue is longest and the seconds are worth most.

4. The payment rails you get tied to
1.75% per in-person sale

Loyalty built into a till system only works with that till system. Square Loyalty runs on Square's POS, which means the loyalty decision and the card-processing decision stop being separate decisions.

Square's published UK rate is 1.75% on in-person transactions with UK cards, and 1.4% + 25p online. That may be perfectly competitive for you — the point is not that it is expensive, it is that you are no longer free to shop around on it. If a processor offers you a better rate in two years, the loyalty scheme is now part of what you would have to move. A loyalty product that works alongside whatever till and card machine you already have leaves that decision open.

5. The price you are not allowed to see
"Book a call"

Some providers publish no pricing at all. Stampede's pricing page describes a flat monthly fee per venue with no commission and no per-cover fees — but the actual figure is "sized to your estate on a quick call".

There may be good reasons for that. The consequence for you is the same either way: you cannot compare it against anything without sitting through a sales call, and a price set on a call is a price that was set with a view of how much you might pay. If you go down that route, get the quote in writing with the renewal price stated, and ask directly what it becomes in year two.

The exit

What it costs to leave

The cost you will not think about for two years, and then think about intensely for a fortnight.

Your customers' progress
The expensive bit

Software migrations are cheap. What is not cheap is telling four hundred regulars that the eight stamps they had collected are gone. That is a goodwill cost with no invoice attached, and it is the single reason most cafés stay on a scheme they have outgrown.

Two questions settle it before you sign, and both are fair to ask of any provider: can I export my customer list, with stamp balances, in a normal spreadsheet, whenever I like? And if I close the account, what happens to that data? A provider that answers both plainly is one you can leave — which, counter-intuitively, is the main reason to trust them with your regulars in the first place. If the answer involves a support ticket and a fee, you have found a switching cost.

Re-training and re-printing
One-off

Smaller and more predictable: an hour per staff member to learn the new flow, new signage for the counter, and a fortnight of explaining the change to every regular. At £12.71 an hour a five-person team is about £64 of training time, plus print. Budget it, but do not let it decide anything — it is roughly one month of the till-time cost in category three.

Questions

Frequently asked

Are there hidden fees with loyalty apps for cafés?

Rarely in the sense of a charge appearing on an invoice you did not expect. The published UK prices are generally what they say. The costs that catch people out are the ones that never appear on an invoice at all: VAT added to a business-to-business price, the tier upgrade needed before the loyalty feature switches on, the card-processing rate you are tied to once loyalty lives inside your till, and above all the staff time each loyalty transaction adds at the counter. That last one is usually the largest single cost of running a loyalty scheme, and no provider will ever bill you for it.

How much does it cost to switch loyalty providers?

In direct cost, very little — an hour of training per staff member, about £64 for a team of five at the April 2026 National Living Wage, plus new counter signage. The real cost is customer goodwill if stamp balances cannot be moved across. Before you sign with anyone, ask whether you can export your customer list and their stamp balances yourself, in a standard spreadsheet, at any time. If you can, switching later is a fortnight of mild inconvenience. If you cannot, you are choosing a provider you will not be able to leave without losing the progress your regulars have built up.

What should a small independent café expect to pay in 2026?

Published UK software pricing spans a wide range: free tiers exist, Square lists loyalty from £25 a month on top of its plan pricing, and Loyalzoo publishes £57 and £127 a month plus VAT. Several providers publish nothing and quote on a call. For a single site, anything above roughly £30 a month should be doing something specific for you that a cheaper option does not — and whatever you pick, the software fee is likely to be a smaller line than the staff time, so weight your decision towards how fast it is at the till.
